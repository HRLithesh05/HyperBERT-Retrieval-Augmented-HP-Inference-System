 Once relevant papers are retrieved, missing values are inferred through structured aggregation:
Categorical values (optimizer, scheduler) → Mode
Continuous values (learning rate, weight decay) → Median
Discrete values (batch size, epochs) → Mode
Every inferred value is assigned a calibrated confidence score and a full reasoning trace linking it back to the supporting papers it came from.

Abstract
A 
retrieval-augmented system
 is designed to address the reproducibility gap in BERT 
fine-tuning research
 caused by incomplete hyperparameter reporting. The system automatically analyzes research papers to detect 
missing
 training configurations, retrieves similar verified studies using semantic vector search, and infers plausible 
hyperparameters
 through structured statistical reasoning. Each inferred value is validated against BERT-specific domain constraints, assigned a calibrated 
confidence score
, and linked to supporting literature. The final output is a ready-to-run annotated 
Jupyter
 Notebook, enabling 
transparent
 and 
reproducible
 experimentation.
Introduction
The Sensitivity Problem BERT fine-tuning is extremely sensitive to hyperparameters. A small change in learning rate, optimizer, batch size, or number of epochs can significantly change the model's performance.
The Gap Despite this, many published papers
:
Omit critical settings like weight decay or scheduler details
Describe hyperparameters vaguely or bury them in appendices
Don't distinguish between Adam and 
AdamW
Leave out warmup steps and training duration entirely
Problem statement
Transformer-based encoder models
 like 
BERT
 are widely used for supervised fine-tuning across diverse NLP tasks, yet their performance is highly sensitive to training 
hyperparameters
 such as learning rate, batch size, optimizer settings, scheduler strategy, and sequence length. In many research papers, these configurations are incompletely reported, ambiguously described, or scattered across sections, making 
exact reproduction difficult
 and leading to significant 
reproducibility gaps 
because even minor hyperparameter differences can alter results. Existing solutions—such as reporting checklists, documentation standards, and code sharing—promote transparency but 
lack
 mechanisms to 
detect missing settings 
or infer plausible values grounded in prior empirical evidence. Therefore, this project addresses 
the need for an adaptive, statistically informed framework 
that can automatically 
identify underspecified hyperparameters 
in BERT fine-tuning studies and reconstruct validated, transparent training configurations to support reliable experimental reproducibility. 
Why Existing Solutions Don't Work?
No Transparent Evidence

AutoML

Asking LLMs gives black-box suggestions with 
no source, no confidence, no traceability

don't adapt 
when evidence is weak
